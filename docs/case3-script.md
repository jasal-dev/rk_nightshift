# Nightshift Case 3: Walk of Fame (Script)

Oct 4, 2026 · @Someone

## Overview

Case 3 runs from 2:45 to 3:50 a.m., about 30 minutes of play, and closes on the night: Pearl Danvers, Gus Lindqvist's assistant, pushed him down his own roof stairs when he caught her selling his collection. It also leaves the optional business card of Walter Brenner, LAPD (Ret.), on the murder board.

**Premise.** Gus Lindqvist, 71, owner of Stardust Memorabilia on Hollywood Boulevard, across from the Chinese Theatre, is found at the foot of the stairs to his roof. The front door's glass is smashed and the shop's prize, Lyle Brandt's 1954 Best Supporting Actor Oscar, is gone from its case. His assistant Pearl found him and called 911 at 2:19. Patrol calls it a robbery.

**Solution.** For eighteen months Pearl has been selling Gus's real pieces through a shell name, "Stardust Archive", and replacing them with copies she signed herself in gold paint pen. Three weeks ago she sold the real Oscar through Calloway's, a Beverly Hills auction house, to a private buyer, and put a prop replica in the case. Tonight Gus read Calloway's new catalogue, found his own Oscar listed as sold, and phoned Pearl at 12:52 a.m. to come in. They argued on the roof, where he smoked his one cigar a night. He grabbed her, tearing off a star earring; she shoved him with both hands and he fell backward down the stairs. Pearl staged a robbery: she opened the case with her key, broke the front door glass from the inside, hid the hollow replica in the old water tank on the roof because the boulevard is covered by cameras, left by the alley and came back through the front at 2:15 to "find" him. The replica floats.

**Flow.** Drive in (Hollywood Boulevard at 3 a.m.) → the shop (Park, Pearl's story, Shah, the broken door, the empty case, Morty at the tape) → the back office (eviction letter and the Brenner card, Gus's phone, the catalogue, the UV lamp, Pearl's locker, the fuse panel) → the roof (neon on, footprints, the earring, the water tank, Charlie on the fire escape) → back to the shop to break Pearl → drive back → squad room (deduction, pins, Doyle's call, Otis rings with Case 4).

The three rooms are connected on foot, so there is no car menu in this case. The roof is dark until the player finds the sign's breaker in the office, which is the case's main gate.

## Script

Six scenes. Scene 1 is a transition; Scenes 2 to 4 are three connected rooms the player can move between freely; Scene 5 is the confrontation back in the shop; Scene 6 returns to the squad room.

### Scene 1. Hollywood Boulevard (2:50 a.m.)

A non-interactive transition, about 20 seconds, skippable. It opens on the title card from the end of Case 2.

*Rain on the windshield. The car turns off Highland onto Hollywood Boulevard. Wet stars in the sidewalk shine under the streetlights. Under the Chinese Theatre's awning, a Marilyn and a Charlie Chaplin share one umbrella, packing their tip jars. A patrol car's lights bounce off a shop window across the street.*

RAY (V.O.): Hollywood Boulevard. Twenty-seven hundred stars in the sidewalk, and everybody walks on them.

*Ray's phone buzzes on the dashboard.*

SHAH (text): stardust. back stairs. bring the good coffee not the squad room coffee

RAY (typing): There's no good coffee at three a.m.

SHAH (text): then just bring yourself

RAY (V.O.): An old man, a staircase, and a missing Oscar. In this town the statue gets top billing.

*Cut to the shop.*

### Scene 2. Stardust Memorabilia, the shop (3:00 a.m.)

**Goal:** hear Pearl's story, and see that the robbery was staged from inside. Introduces Pearl, Morty and the UV lamp's main target.

*INT. STARDUST MEMORABILIA. NIGHT.*

*A long, narrow shop crammed floor to ceiling: glass counters of props and costume jewelry, a rack of studio jackets, a wall of signed photographs in cheap gold frames. In the middle, on a pedestal, a tall glass case lined in red velvet, empty, the velvet still dented in the shape of a statue. A framed certificate hangs beside it. At the front, the door's glass panel is smashed; rain blows through the hole. OFFICER PARK stands at the door. Outside under the awning, behind the tape, MORTY KAHN in a bathrobe and loafers. PEARL DANVERS sits in a canvas director's chair stenciled STARDUST, a patrol blanket around her shoulders, auburn hair down over the left side of her face. A curtained doorway at the back leads to the office.*

RAY: Stardust Memorabilia. Sixty years of other people's dreams, sold by the square inch.

PARK: Detective. Small city tonight.

RAY: It always is, after midnight.

PARK: Gus Lindqvist, seventy-one, the owner. He's in back, at the bottom of the roof stairs. Dr. Shah's with him. The 911 call came in at two-nineteen from his assistant, Pearl Danvers. *(she nods at the chair)* I got here at two twenty-six. The door was like that, and the case was empty.

**Talking to Park.**

[choice] Who's the man in the bathrobe? / Anything from the canvass? / The developer's man. (after clue\_eviction) / That'll do.

*Who's the man in the bathrobe?*

PARK: Morty Kahn. Owns the collectibles shop three doors down. He came out when he saw the lights. He keeps asking if "it" is gone. I don't think he means Mr. Lindqvist.

*Anything from the canvass?*

PARK: The boulevard's shut. There's a Charlie Chaplin who lives next door. He's out on his fire escape, and he says he'll only talk to "the man in charge."

RAY: That's never me.

PARK: Tonight it is. You can see his fire escape from Mr. Lindqvist's roof.

*The developer's man.* (after clue\_eviction)

RAY: There's an eviction notice on Gus's desk from Pryce Development. Their man's Trent Whitaker. Where was he tonight?

PARK: Already asked. Miss Danvers mentioned him. Pryce has a night security desk; the supervisor sent me stills in four minutes. Whitaker's car went into the Pryce Tower garage in Century City at ten fifty-eight. He's on their elevator camera at three-oh-two a.m., still in his tie.

RAY: Four minutes. Nobody's that helpful at three in the morning.

PARK: He said they "always cooperate with the department." Like he'd said it before. *(sets clue\_whitaker\_alibi)*

*That'll do.*

PARK: I'll keep the door, Detective. Mr. Kahn keeps trying to come in.

**Talking to Pearl.** The first time:

PEARL: Are you the detective? They keep saying "the detective's coming," like it's a casting call. Sorry. I'm sorry. I don't know what I'm saying.

RAY: Ray Kessler. Take your time, Miss Danvers.

PEARL: Pearl. Gus calls me Pearlie. *(beat)* Called.

[choice] What happened tonight? / How long did you work for Gus? / Who has keys to the Oscar case? / Who'd want the Oscar? / (Scene 5 options, once their evidence is found) / I'll be back.

*What happened tonight?*

PEARL: We closed at nine. I went home. I've got an audition at nine this morning, a pharmacy ad, "concerned mom", and I left my headshots in my locker. So I came back. Two-fifteen, maybe? The door was smashed. Lyle was gone. And Gus was at the bottom of the stairs. I called 911.

RAY: Two in the morning, for headshots.

PEARL: You don't know casting directors. *(sets clue\_pearl\_story)*

*How long did you work for Gus?*

PEARL: Six years. I came in to sell him a lobby card from my grandmother's attic, and he gave me a job instead. Said I had an eye. He trusted me with everything. *(beat)* Everything.

*Who has keys to the Oscar case?*

PEARL: Gus has one. I have one. *(she lifts a small brass key on her lanyard)* That's it. Gus didn't let anyone else near Lyle.

RAY: Lyle?

PEARL: Lyle Brandt. The Oscar. Gus called it by name. "Morning, Lyle. Night, Lyle." *(sets clue\_keyholders)*

*Who'd want the Oscar?*

PEARL: Everybody. Morty out there's been begging Gus for years. And the developer. Pryce. Their man Whitaker was here yesterday yelling that Gus had thirty days. Gus told him to put it in writing, and he said it already was.

*I'll be back.*

PEARL: I'm not going anywhere. *(she looks at the empty case)* Where would I go?

**Puzzle 2.1: Broken in, or broken out?** Two observations, in either order.

Look at or use the front door: "The glass in the door is smashed. Most of it is out on the sidewalk, on the star of a TV cowboy nobody remembers." Ray: "Break into a shop and the glass lands inside. This glass went out." *(sets clue\_glass\_out)*

Use the display case: "The lock's clean. No scratches, no pry marks, and the glass is whole. Somebody opened this with a key." *(sets clue\_key\_opened)* Using gus\_keys on it: "The little brass key turns like butter. Same kind Pearl wears around her neck."

Once both are set, Ray puts them together:

RAY: Glass out on the sidewalk. The case opened with a key. Nobody broke in here. Somebody with a key broke out, and wanted it to look the other way. *(sets clue\_staged)*

**Puzzle 2.2: Two of the same.** Needs the catalogue from the office (Scene 3). Use catalogue on the certificate:

RAY: Calloway's: "Base serial No. 734. Sold by private treaty." Three weeks ago. Gus's certificate: No. 734. Every Oscar since 1949 has its own number, like a badge.

RAY: One statue, two places. Either Calloway's sold a fake, or Gus was saying good night to one. *(sets clue\_same\_serial)*

RAY: And since 1950, winners sign a paper: if you sell it, the Academy gets first refusal for a dollar. Nobody sells one in public. That's why it went quiet.

**Puzzle 2.3: The wall of fame.** Needs uv\_lamp from the office. Gus's note on the lamp (see Scene 3) tells the player what it does. Use uv\_lamp on the signed photo wall:

**Talking to Morty** (through the broken door; he stays outside the tape).

MORTY: Is it gone? Tell me it's not gone. *(to Park)* I'm not going anywhere, young lady. I've known Gus fifty years.

[choice] Who are you? / You wanted the Oscar. / Where were you tonight? / Calloway's sold No. 734. (with clue\_same\_serial, or use catalogue on Morty) / What does a real one weigh? (after clue\_morty\_bought) / Good night, Morty.

*Who are you?*

MORTY: Morty Kahn. Kahn's Collectibles, three doors down. Gus and I have been fighting over the same estate sales since Nixon.

*You wanted the Oscar.*

MORTY: Wanted it? In March I offered him two hundred fifty thousand dollars. Cash. He said he'd be buried with it. *(beat)* Stubborn old Swede. *(sets clue\_morty\_offer)*

*Where were you tonight?*

MORTY: Upstairs, in my pajamas, losing a Kurosawa script to a dentist in Osaka. Look.

*He holds up his phone. The device screen shows Yamato Online Auctions, bid history: 12:31 a.m., 12:58, 1:20, 1:44, 2:09 "You have been outbid."*

MORTY: Two hours of my life. I came down when I saw the lights. *(sets clue\_morty\_alibi)*

**Puzzle 2.4: Morty's secret.** *Calloway's sold No. 734.*

RAY: Calloway's private sales, three weeks ago. Lyle Brandt's Oscar, serial number 734. Somebody bought it, Morty.

*Morty pulls the bathrobe tighter. A long beat.*

MORTY: It was me. All right? Me. A broker at Calloway's called. A collection selling quietly, no names. I wired a hundred and eighty thousand to something called Stardust Archive.

RAY: And you thought?

MORTY: I thought the eviction finally broke him. Too proud to sell to me to my face, so I let him not. I never said a word. Fifty years, and I never said a word. *(beat)* It's in my safe right now, Detective. It's real. I'd know.

RAY: Who handles the shop's email?

MORTY: Gus? Gus thought email was a fad. The girl does it. Pearl. The website, the listings, everything with a screen. *(sets clue\_morty\_bought)*

*What does a real one weigh?*

MORTY: Eight and a half pounds. Solid britannium, gold plate. You could stop a door with it. The props they sell on this boulevard are hollow resin, two pounds. They'd float in your bathtub. *(sets clue\_weight)*

*Good night, Morty.*

MORTY: Find who did this. And whatever was in that case, I want to know what it was.

### Scene 3. The back office (3:10 a.m.)

**Goal:** learn how Gus died, find what he found tonight, and turn on the roof. Introduces the UV lamp, the phone log and the seed.

*INT. STARDUST, BACK OFFICE. NIGHT.*

*A small, warm, cluttered room. A rolltop desk under a green banker's lamp, buried in paper. A gray filing cabinet. On the wall, an old fuse panel with hand-written labels. Two tall metal staff lockers, one marked GUS, one marked PEARL, the PEARL door ajar. A cordless phone in its cradle. Steep wooden stairs climb to a roof door at the top, propped open, rain drifting down. GUS LINDQVIST lies at the foot of the stairs in a cardigan, one slipper on, face turned to the wall. DR. SHAH kneels beside him.*

RAY: Gus's office. Everything he ever loved, filed under "later."

*Ray is already snapping on gloves.*

SHAH: Look at that. You can be taught.

RAY: You've been saying that for eleven years.

**Talking to Shah.**

[choice] How did he die? / When? / Did he fall, or was he helped? (after How) / Anything else? / Thanks, Anita.

*How did he die?*

SHAH: Backward down fourteen wooden stairs. Skull fracture at the bottom, neck too. He'd have been gone before he stopped moving.

SHAH: Here. His keys were in his cardigan. I'm done with them. *(gives gus\_keys)*

*When?*

SHAH: Between one-fifteen and one-forty-five. The roof door's been letting the rain in on him, so don't hold me to the minute. *(sets clue\_tod)*

*Did he fall, or was he helped?*

SHAH: He had a nice face. Find out who he made it at, last.

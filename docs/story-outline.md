# Nightshift: Story Outline

Oct 3, 2026 · @Someone

## Premise

One rainy night shift, 12:15 a.m. to sunrise, in present-day Los Angeles. Homicide detective Ray Kessler works five deaths in a row, and the first and last turn out to be the same case.

The night opens on Danny Reyes, a piano player found dead behind the Blue Note two nights ago. Ray clears the obvious suspect, but the case doesn't close. Three unrelated calls pull him across the city, each a short, self-contained mystery. Each one also leaves a small detail that only makes sense at dawn, when the bartender who helped him is found dead and Danny's case comes back with a name.

**Tone:** hard-boiled but modern. Dry first-person narration, rain, sodium light, neon, phones and dashcams instead of typewriters. The city is a character: Hollywood, Koreatown, the LA River, the port. The crimes are small and human; the conspiracy behind Danny is about money and real estate, not mobsters.

**Theme:** who stays up for the dead. Nobody important cares about a piano player, a rideshare driver or a man in a river encampment. Ray does, and the night tests what that costs him.

This outline builds on the vertical slice already in the project: the squad room, the street outside the Blue Note, Sal the bartender, the matchbook number 555-0147, the knock, and Sal's tip about Vance at Pier 9.

## Cast

**Ray Kessler**, 52, Detective III, Hollywood Homicide, Room 214. Twenty-six years on the job, the last eight on permanent night shift because he asked for it. Former Army MP (Iraq, 2003). Divorced; his daughter Maya is at UC Santa Cruz and texts him at odd hours, which gives the player a few quiet beats. Loves jazz, which is why the Blue Note case got under his skin. Wears the same raincoat for twenty years and it still leaks at the collar. His rule: *somebody has to stay up for them.* He is patient, stubborn, sarcastic with superiors and gentle with witnesses.

| Character | Role | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lt. Maureen Doyle | Ray's boss | Wants Reyes closed by Friday. Not corrupt, but loyal to the wrong man. Calls Ray throughout the night. |
| Sgt. Otis Bell | Night desk sergeant | The voice on the desk phone that sends Ray each new case. Warm, funny, tired. |
| Dr. Anita Shah | Coroner's investigator | Meets Ray at scenes 2 to 5. Gives the forensic hint when the player is stuck. |

| Walt Brenner | Retired LAPD, now "security consultant" | Doyle's old training officer. Works for Pryce. The real killer. |

## The shift

Five cases, about 2 h 30 min in total. Case 1 doubles as the tutorial and case 5 as the finale, so both run longer; the three middle cases are 25 to 30 minutes each. The squad room is the hub Ray returns to between cases, where the desk phone rings with the next call and the murder board fills up.

| Time | Case | Where | Crime | Playtime | Rooms |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 12:15 a.m. | 1. Dead Piano Player | Blue Note, Hollywood | Danny Reyes shot in the alley | 35 min | Squad room, street, Pier 9 dock, Vance's office |
| 1:40 a.m. | 2. Five Stars | Mulholland overlook | Rideshare driver dead in his car | 25 min | Overlook, Kenji and Devin's apartment, Norm's diner |
| 2:45 a.m. | 3. Walk of Fame | Hollywood Blvd memorabilia shop | Shop owner killed, a prop Oscar stolen | 30 min | Shop, back office, rooftop |
| 3:50 a.m. | 4. Low Water | LA River, Elysian Valley | Body under a bridge in the dry channel | 25 min | Bridge, river channel, Los Feliz house, Crane's garage |
| 4:50 a.m. | 5. Last Call | Blue Note, then Pier 9 | Sal found hanged in the closed bar | 40 min | Blue Note bar, back room, Pier 9 at dawn |

The clock advances only at the end of each case, so there is no time pressure in play. Between cases Ray drives (a short rain-on-windshield transition with one line of narration) and Lt. Doyle calls to ask why Reyes isn't closed yet.

## The cases

Every case is solved on the night except the first, which stays on the murder board until dawn. Each one ends with a short deduction scene where the player picks the culprit and the two pieces of evidence that prove it.

### Case 1: Dead Piano Player (12:15 a.m.)

**Setup.** Danny Reyes, 34, piano player at the Blue Note, was found in the alley two nights ago, shot once with a .38. Wallet still on him; his phone was never found. Lt. Doyle wants it closed by Friday as a debt killing.

**Investigation.** The existing slice: key under the lamp, the file, the dime in the coffee, the matchbook, 555-0147 and the knock. Sal talks through the door and names Vance at Pier 9. New part: Ray drives to the port, gets past Vance's doorman (a small puzzle: the doorman drank at the Blue Note, and the matchbook plus Danny's knock gets Ray a hearing), and questions Vance in a container office.

**Twist.** Vance didn't do it, and he can prove it: "Dead men don't pay." Danny paid him off in full last Tuesday, $8,000 in cash, in a Blue Note envelope. On the back of the envelope Danny wrote "1 of 3." A broke piano player suddenly had money, and his phone is missing while his wallet wasn't touched.

**Resolution.** Ray's deduction: Danny wasn't killed for money he owed but for something he had. He pins the envelope to the board next to Danny's photo. The case stays open. Back in the squad room, Ray calls Doyle to report; she tells him to stop wasting the night. Then the desk phone rings.

### Case 2: Five Stars (1:40 a.m.)

**Setup.** Kenji Ota, 29, rideshare driver, found dead behind the wheel at a Mulholland overlook, engine running, the dashcam mount empty.

**Suspects.** His last passenger, a drunk influencer dropped in Silver Lake at 11:52 p.m.; his roommate Devin Clark; a rival driver who'd threatened him over a stolen airport queue spot.

**Clues.** The app shows the last ride ended in Silver Lake, but the car's own GPS shows he drove 20 minutes to Mulholland afterwards, off the app, so he was with someone he knew. Two coffee cups from Norm's, the all-night diner, in the cup holders. The dashcam was unscrewed neatly, not ripped out. The diner waitress remembers Kenji coming in with "his roommate, the one with the camera bag."

**Solution.** Kenji and Devin sold dashcam clips of celebrity passengers to a gossip site. Kenji wanted to stop after a clip got a woman fired; Devin killed him for the memory cards. The SD cards are in Devin's freezer, wrapped in a bag of frozen peas.

**Seed.** Ray scrolls Kenji's ride history to check the timeline. Two nights ago at 1:10 a.m.: pickup outside the Blue Note, drop-off at Pryce Tower, Century City. Passenger "Walt B.", rated one star, with Kenji's note: "wet, rude, smelled like gun oil." Ray only says, "Blue Note. Two nights ago. Huh," and the ride receipt goes on the board.

### Case 3: Walk of Fame (2:45 a.m.)

**Setup.** Gus Lindqvist, 71, owner of Stardust Memorabilia on Hollywood Boulevard, found at the foot of the stairs to his roof. The prize of his shop, a 1954 Best Supporting Actor Oscar, is gone from its case.

**Suspects.** His assistant Pearl Danvers, a struggling actress who found the body; Morty Kahn, a rival collector who'd begged to buy the Oscar; and an agent from Pryce Development who has been trying to evict Gus for months.

**Clues.** The display case was opened with a key, not forced. The eviction letter on Gus's desk makes the developer look guilty, but its delivery stamp is a week old and the agent was on camera at a Century City garage all night. The auction house's catalogue in the back office lists the same Oscar as sold privately three weeks ago. Pearl's audition headshots in her locker are signed with the same gold pen used on several "signed" photos in the shop.

**Solution.** Pearl had been selling the real pieces and replacing them with fakes she signed herself. The Oscar in the case was already a replica. Gus found the auction listing tonight, confronted her on the roof, and fell during the struggle. She took the fake Oscar to stage a robbery. It's on the roof, inside an old water tank.

**Seed.** On Gus's desk, under the eviction letter, is a business card: "Walter Brenner, LAPD (Ret.), Security Consultant, Pryce Development." A neighbor tells Ray that Brenner came by last week and said the whole block would be empty by Christmas, "the jazz club too." The card goes on the board.

### Case 4: Low Water (3:50 a.m.)

**Setup.** Owen Tate, 24, a food delivery rider, found in the concrete LA River channel under the Fletcher Drive bridge. Uniforms have already cuffed "Preacher," an Army veteran from the riverside encampment, because Owen's bike was at his tent.

**Suspects.** Preacher; Owen's last customer, who complained about a late order; Elliot Crane, chief of staff to City Councilman Ted Haskell, whose car turns up later in the case.

**Clues.** Preacher says he found the bike on the bridge and dragged it down to keep it from being stolen. Dr. Shah finds Owen's injuries match a car, not a beating. Blue-gray paint flakes and a shard of headlight lens are on the bike. The delivery app shows Owen's last drop-off at a hillside house in Los Feliz, where a fundraiser was ending. The valet there remembers a guest who left drunk in a blue-gray Audi.

**Solution.** Crane hit Owen on the bridge while driving home drunk from the fundraiser, then rolled the body down the embankment so it would look like an encampment killing. The broken headlight puzzle closes it: Ray matches the lens shard to Crane's car, parked in his garage with a fresh tarp over the front.

**Seed.** Two parts. The fundraiser was Harlan Pryce's, for Councilman Haskell, whose vote on rezoning the Blue Note block comes up next week. And Preacher, grateful, tells Ray that two nights ago "a big man in an old cop's coat" threw something small and black off this same bridge. Searching the channel for the headlight shard, the player also finds a phone in a cracked case with a Blue Note sticker: Danny's. Ray drops it at the night lab with Ike, the tech, who says he'll try.

### Case 5: Last Call (4:50 a.m.)

**Setup.** Otis calls: Sal Moretti is dead, hanging from a pipe in the Blue Note's back room. It looks like suicide. Ray knows it isn't, and knows he is the reason Sal talked.

**Investigation.** First time inside the Blue Note. No forced entry, and Sal opened his door only to Danny's knock. Who knew it? Ray, Nina, and whoever Nina told. Nina is found hiding in the walk-in cooler, terrified. She admits a man called her around 2 a.m. from a police number, said he was Ray's partner, and asked for the knock. Dr. Shah confirms Sal was dead before the rope: the bruising is from a forearm choke, a police hold. Sal's bar tab book has a last entry in his hand: "W.B., club soda, 3:20."

**The leak.** Ray checks his own call log. The only person he told about Sal was Lt. Doyle, at 1:30 a.m. The police number that called Nina was Doyle's desk line. Doyle didn't make the call herself; she phoned her old training officer, Walt Brenner, who was in her office "dropping off coffee" and used her phone.

**The recording.** Ike calls: Danny's phone is dead, but it was backing up to the cloud. The account password is Danny's knock written as notes on the Blue Note set list (puzzle using the piano on stage). The last file is a "practice take" from the night he died. Under the piano you can hear the back booth: Pryce, Councilman Haskell and Brenner, cash for the rezoning vote, and Brenner saying, "The piano player was close enough to hear." Danny had been blackmailing them; the $8,000 he paid Vance was the first of three payments.

**Finale.** Ray sets a meet at Pier 9 at dawn, using Danny's phone as bait. Brenner comes alone and tries the old-cop pitch: the city runs on deals like this, Doyle owes him her career, Ray can retire comfortably. Vance from case 1 is there because it's his pier, which gives Ray a witness and a way out when Brenner draws the .38. The confrontation is dialogue-driven, with the choice between talking Brenner down and forcing his hand.

## The connecting thread

The truth behind Danny is a rezoning bribe. Harlan Pryce wants the Blue Note block for a tower; Councilman Haskell's vote is for sale; Walt Brenner handles the problems. Danny recorded the deal by accident from the piano, tried to sell it back to them, and was shot by Brenner in the alley. Brenner took the phone and threw it in the river.

The middle cases never mention Danny directly. Each leaves one object on the murder board, and by case 5 the string between them points at one man.

| Found in | Board item | What it proves at dawn | Required? |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Case 1 | Envelope "1 of 3" | Danny was blackmailing someone, not just in debt | Yes |
| Case 2 | Ride receipt, "Walt B.", Blue Note to Pryce Tower, 1:10 a.m. | Brenner left the Blue Note right after the shooting | No |
| Case 3 | Brenner's business card | Brenner works for Pryce and wants the block empty | No |
| Case 4 | Pryce fundraiser for Haskell | Pryce is paying Haskell | No |
| Case 4 | Danny's phone from the river | The recording exists | Yes |
| Case 5 | Sal's tab book, "W.B.", and Doyle's desk line | Brenner killed Sal, and got the knock through Doyle | Yes |

The optional seeds decide how strong Ray's case is in the finale (see Endings). A player who misses them still finishes the story, but Ray goes into Pier 9 knowing less. Seeds are deliberately quiet: one line from Ray, one pin on the board, no music sting.

## Endings

Brenner is arrested in every ending; what changes is who else falls and what it costs Ray. After Pier 9, Ray holds the only copy of the recording and chooses where it goes.

| Choice | Needs | Outcome |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Lt. Doyle | All three optional seeds | Ray lays out the full board. Doyle realizes how Brenner used her, and testifies. Pryce and Haskell are indicted. Best ending. |
| Lt. Doyle | Fewer than three seeds | Doyle takes the phone "to IA" and it disappears. Brenner pleads to Sal's murder alone; the tower gets built. Bitter ending. |
| Internal Affairs and the DA | Nothing | By the book. Brenner is charged, Haskell resigns months later, Pryce's lawyers stall. Doyle is reassigned. |
| An LA Times reporter | Nothing | The story breaks by noon. Pryce's deal dies, but Ray is suspended for the leak. |

The last scene is the same in all endings: the rain stops, the sun comes up over the port, and Ray reads a text from Maya, "you up?", and answers, "Always." The Blue Note's piano is heard over the credits.

## Decisions

- **Payphone stays.** The dime-operated payphone is part of the night's noir flavor and needs no modern swap.
- **Deductions allow retries.** A wrong accusation gets a line from Ray and the player picks again; there's no penalty.
- **Best ending needs every seed.** The Doyle ending requires all three optional seeds, which rewards careful play and replays.

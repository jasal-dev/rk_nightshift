# Nightshift: Case 5 Script (Last Call)

Oct 4, 2026 · @Someone

## Overview

Case 5 runs from 4:50 a.m. to sunrise, about 45 minutes of play, and closes the night: Walt Brenner, retired LAPD and Harlan Pryce's driver, strangled Sal Moretti in the Blue Note and staged a hanging, after getting Danny's knock from Nina on Lt. Doyle's desk phone. Danny's last recording proves Brenner killed Danny too, for Pryce's bribe to Councilman Haskell. Ray arrests Brenner at Pier 9 at dawn, then decides who gets the recording, which picks one of four endings.

**Premise.** At 4:30 a.m. the Blue Note's porter came in to mop and found Sal Moretti hanging from a pipe in the back room. The uniforms are calling it a suicide. Three hours ago Sal talked to Ray through his door, because Ray knocked like Danny Reyes. Ray knows Sal didn't kill himself, and knows he is the reason Sal talked.

**Solution.** At 1:30 a.m. Ray told Lt. Doyle, on her office line one floor up, that Sal opens his door to Danny's knock and that Nina gave it to him. Walt Brenner, Doyle's old training officer, was in her office with coffee and heard Doyle's half of it, which named Sal Moretti but never the knock or Nina. When she hung up he asked, the way her old training officer would, and she told him the rest like shop talk. At 1:50 Doyle went down the hall to the copier. Brenner used her desk phone to call Nina, said he was Ray's partner, and asked for the knock, so Nina's caller ID showed a police number and his own phone showed nothing. At 3:15 he knocked two slow, three fast on the Blue Note's door. Sal let him in, poured him a club soda and wrote it in his tab book, as he wrote down every drink. Brenner wanted to know what Danny had left Sal. He opened Danny's locker with Sal's keys, found sheet music, and at about 3:40 killed Sal from behind with a bar-arm choke, a hold the LAPD banned in 1982. He hung Sal from the pipe, washed his glass, and left by the front door at about 4:00, which locked on the latch instead of the deadbolt. Nina, who had come in the back way at 3:30 to check on Sal, heard all of it from inside the walk-in cooler and hasn't come out since, because the man said he was police.

**Behind it.** On Tuesday night the Blue Note closed to the public at eleven for a private booking: Pryce, Haskell and Brenner in the back booth, with Danny paid to play late. Two weeks earlier Danny had overheard their first meeting and sold his silence: $8,000 on Monday, two more payments to come (the envelope "1 of 3"). On Tuesday he recorded the second meeting on his phone as a "practice take". On it Pryce hands Haskell the second $50,000 of a bribe for the Hollywood Core vote, Brenner says the piano player is close enough to hear, and Pryce says, "Then pay him, Walt." Danny stepped out for a smoke at 11:52. Brenner shot him in the alley at about midnight and took his phone. Then he did what an old cop does: he stayed. He stood at the tape with the uniforms for an hour, a helpful retired officer, the one person at a murder scene nobody looks at twice, and made sure they never wondered about a phone. At 1:10 Pryce's office booked him a Glide on the company account, from the Blue Note to Pryce Tower, so nothing went on his own card; Kenji Ota's cabin camera filmed him in the back seat with Danny's phone in his lap. Pryce couldn't unlock the phone either. After 3 a.m., driving Pryce's gray Lincoln home to Glassell Park, Brenner threw it off the Fletcher Drive bridge into a river running high after the rain, expecting it to wash out to sea. The phone never mattered: the take had already uploaded to the cloud.

**Flow.** Across the street (crime tape, the reporter, the porter, Park) → the Blue Note bar (the front door, the tab book, the wet glass, the piano and set list) → the back room (Shah and Sal, the stool, Danny's locker, the back hall, Nina in the cooler) → Room 214 (the desk phone's call log, the phone list, the 1:30 call remembered, Otis's visitor log, Doyle's call, Ike's call) → back to the piano (the knock as notes, the password, the take) → Room 214 (final deduction, the string, Danny's box, calls to Vance and Otis, the text to Brenner) → the lab and the drive → Pier 9 at dawn (Vance, Tiny, Brenner, three lies, the .38) → sunrise (where the recording goes) → the last scene and the credits.

The street, the bar, the back room and Room 214 all connect on foot, since the Blue Note is across the street from the station. There are four gates: Nina out of the cooler, the leak traced to Doyle's office, the password, and the final deduction. Pier 9 is a single scene with no way back.

### Scene 1. Across the street (4:52 a.m.)

**Goal:** get past the tape and into the Blue Note. Meets Mara Quist (the reporter the Times ending needs) and Teo, who explains the door.

It opens on the title card from the end of Case 4. Ray comes out of the precinct door into the street.

EXT. STREET OUTSIDE THE PRECINCT. NIGHT. RAIN EASING.

*The street from Case 1, changed. A patrol car with its lights turning sits half on the curb. The coroner's van is parked behind it. Yellow tape runs from the streetlamp to the hydrant and across the Blue Note's door, which stands open with light spilling out. The BLUE NOTE neon is dark. OFFICER LENA PARK stands at the door in her rain cape. TEO ALCARAZ, the porter, sits on the curb under the awning with a mop bucket beside him, staring at nothing. At the tape, MARA QUIST, raincoat and notebook, is already waiting.*

RAY: Three hours ago I stood here and knocked like a dead man. Sal opened up.

RAY: The neon's off. Eight years across the street, and I've never seen it off.

**Mara at the tape.** Plays automatically the first time Ray walks toward the Blue Note.

MARA: Detective Kessler? Mara Quist, the Times. I heard it on the scanner. Same bar as the piano player Tuesday. Is that a pattern?

RAY: It's a bar. People keep dying near it.

MARA: That's what a pattern is.

[choice] No comment. / How do you know my name? / Go home, Ms. Quist.

No comment.

MARA: You'd be amazed how many stories start with that.

How do you know my name?

MARA: You're the only detective in Hollywood who works nights on purpose. People talk about you. Not always kindly.

Go home, Ms. Quist.

MARA: I am home. I live on the scanner.

All three end the same way. She holds out a card.

MARA: When it's a pattern, call me. Not the press office. Me. (gives reporter\_card; sets got\_reporter\_card)

After that, talking to Mara:

[choice] What are you working on? / What do you know about the block? / Goodnight.

What are you working on?

MARA: City Hall. Who pays for whose vote. It's like homicide, but nobody ever gets caught.

What do you know about the block?

MARA: Hollywood Core. Pryce Development wants forty stories where this bar is. The council votes next Wednesday. Ted Haskell's the swing vote, and nobody can find out which way he swings. I've been on it a year. Nobody will talk to me. (sets clue\_mara\_beat)

With saw\_rezoning, Ray glances at the notice on the wall. RAY: "Pryce Development. It was taped to the Blue Note's wall the whole time."

Goodnight.

MARA: It isn't. But thanks.

**Talking to Park.**

PARK: Detective. Third time tonight. They should give us a punch card.

[choice] What happened? / Who cut him down? / What's patrol calling it? / Danny's alley, Tuesday. / That'll do.

What happened?

PARK: The porter found him at four thirty-one and called it in on the bar phone. I was two blocks away. He's in the back room. Dr. Shah's with him.

Who cut him down?

PARK: Me. To check for a pulse. There wasn't one. (beat) I'm sorry, Detective. Otis said you knew him.

What's patrol calling it?

PARK: The watch commander says suicide. Front door locked, back door locked, a man alone in his own bar with a rope.

RAY: A note?

PARK: No note.

RAY: Sal wrote down every drink he poured for thirty years. And he didn't leave a note. (sets clue\_no\_note)

Danny's alley, Tuesday.

PARK: I held his tape. Midnight to four, in the rain.

RAY: Anybody hang around?

PARK: The usual. Two drunks and a man with a dog. And a retired cop. Big old-timer, white hair, belted raincoat. He showed me his old ID and stood inside the tape with us for an hour like he never left the job. Held it up for the coroner's van. Brought us all coffee from the place on Cahuenga.

RAY: He ask anything?

PARK: Whether we'd found the victim's phone. I said no. He said kids today lose everything. A car came for him around ten past one.

RAY: Name?

PARK: Walt something. He said everybody at Hollywood knows him. (beat) It's in my scene log. I log everybody inside my tape.

RAY: An old cop at a fresh scene, asking about the one thing that's missing. (sets clue\_tape\_helper)

That'll do.

PARK: I'll keep the Times on the right side of the tape. She's very polite about ignoring me.

**Talking to Teo.** The first time:

TEO: You're the detective from across the street. Sal said you came by. Through the door.

RAY: I did.

TEO: He was scared after. He told me, "Teo, I talked." Like it was a sin.

[choice] You found him? / The front door. / Sal's habits. / Who knows the knock? / Go home, Teo.

You found him?

TEO: Four-thirty, like every morning. Nineteen years. We've been closed since Danny, but Sal said keep coming, we open again Saturday. I let myself in, I take the chairs down, I go to the back for the mop. And he was... (beat) I called 911 on the bar phone. Then I came out here. I haven't been able to go back in.

The front door.

TEO: It was on the latch. Just the latch. Sal threw the deadbolt every night, and the chain, and he checked it twice. Nineteen years, I never once came in on just the latch.

RAY: Somebody went out the front and pulled it shut behind him. (sets clue\_latch)

Sal's habits.

TEO: He wrote everything down. Every drink, in the book by the register. Even mine, and I don't drink. "Teo, water, four thirty-five," every morning. He used to say, "If it isn't in the book, it didn't happen." (sets clue\_tab\_habit)

Who knows the knock?

TEO: I don't knock. I have a key. Since Tuesday Sal only opened that door to Danny's knock. He said, "Teo, if anybody knocks it any other way, I'm not home." (sets clue\_knock\_only)

Go home, Teo.

TEO: Who's going to mop?

RAY: Not tonight.

## Continuity facts

Sal Moretti, 64, tended the Blue Note for 31 years and leased it; he refused Pryce's buyout of the last six years of his lease. He was killed at about 3:40 a.m. Friday with a bar-arm choke (banned by the LAPD in 1982) and hung from a pipe in the back room; Officer Lena Park cut him down. The Blue Note has been closed since Danny died; Sal stayed in the dark bar at night and meant to reopen Saturday. On Tuesday it closed to the public at 11:00 for Pryce's private booking, with Danny paid to play late. Teo Alcaraz, the porter, has keys and has mopped at 4:30 every morning for 19 years. The Blue Note is across the street from the Hollywood station; Doyle's office is one floor above Room 214, direct line (323) 555-0186, extension 2186; the front desk is extension 1000. Walter "Walt" Brenner was LAPD from 1979 to 2009, mostly Rampart, Doyle's training officer, now security consultant and driver for Pryce Development; cell 213-555-0163; he drinks club soda and carries a .38 revolver. He signed in at the front desk at 1:20 a.m. and out at 2:05; Doyle was at the copier from 1:50 to 1:56; he called Nina at 1:52.
